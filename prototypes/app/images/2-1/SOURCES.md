**Q1. Morris, *Pimpernel*: the photograph.**
- Commons (read 9 October 2026): Google Art Project file; its record gives "core:rights = Ministerio de Educación, Cultura
  y Deporte. España" and the museum record http://ceres.mcu.es/pages/Main?idt=18281&inventary=CE22112&table=FMUS&museum=MNAD;
  Commons tags it PD-Art (PD-old-100).
- Spain: a simple photograph (*mera fotografía*) has a 25-year right (art. 128 of the Ley de Propiedad Intelectual)
  (search). Real Decreto-ley 24/2021 (BOE-A-2021-17910), art. 72, "Obras de arte visual de dominio público", transposes
  Article 14 of Directive (EU) 2019/790: once the term of a work of visual art has expired, material resulting from its
  reproduction is not subject to intellectual-property rights unless it is original (search: Cuatrecasas,
  https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/spain/propiedad-intelectual/art/derecho-imagen-sobre-bienes-culturales-acceso-dominio-publico;
  Generalitat de Catalunya comparison table). The BOE text was not opened.
- Not settled: whether this also ends the right in a photograph made before the rule came into force. The Directive's
  Article 26(2) applies "without prejudice to any acts concluded and rights acquired before 7 June 2021" (search, from a
  copy of the EUR-Lex text); Communia reads Article 14 as covering earlier reproductions as well
  (https://communia-association.org/2019/12/11/implementing-new-eu-provision-protects-public-domain/). No source found
  resolves the two.
- Finding: the work is in the public domain everywhere; the photograph's status in Spain is not settled. OPEN.
- Recommendation: a museum CC0 image of another Morris wallpaper (see "Documented alternatives"). Not replaced.

**Q3. Sophie Taeuber-Arp in the United States.**
- Outside the US: she died on 13 January 1943, so her works are in the public domain where the term is life + 70
  (Switzerland, the EU, Israel) from 1 January 2014.
- US rules (Commons, "URAA-restored copyrights", read 9 October 2026; 17 U.S.C. § 104A): the US restored copyright in
  foreign works that were still protected in their source country on 1 January 1996. In Switzerland and France the term
  was then life + 70, so her works were protected then. A restored work first published 1931–1977 is protected 95 years
  from publication; first published 1978–2002, until the end of 2047; not published before 2003, its term ended with
  life + 70 (end of 2013); published in 1930 or earlier, it is in the public domain.
- Rights are claimed in the US: the Buffalo AKG Art Museum's record of no. 3 gives "Estate of Sophie Taeuber-Arp /
  Artists Rights Society (ARS), New York / VG Bild-Kunst, Germany" and allows image downloads for educational use only
  (search, two searches; the AMICA record of 1969:12 gives ARS / VG Bild-Kunst too). Gallery listings credit her works
  "© Stiftung Arp e.V., Berlin/Rolandswerth / Artists Rights Society (ARS), New York" (search). So the estate treats her
  works as protected in the US.
- Publication: she was a member of Abstraction-Création from 1931, whose yearly cahiers (Paris, 1932–1936) reproduced
  members' works (search: https://monoskop.org/Abstraction-creation); the 1948 monograph (Georg Schmidt, ed., Basel)
  carries the Schmidt/Weber catalogue cited in the table (1930/5, 1933/10, 1934/1). No. 4 was shown at the Kunsthalle
  Basel in 1937 (search). Which of the six works were reproduced, and when first, has not been established.
- Commons keeps these files with a public-domain tag for the painting and no US tag. Its URAA page says a URAA claim
  alone is not a reason to delete; so their being on Commons is not evidence of their US status.
- Fair use (17 U.S.C. § 107) is decided case by case and is not a clearance. The home page offers the programme to
  schools ("לשאלות, להצעת מחיר או לתיאום הדגמה"), which is relevant to it. Not assessed.
- Finding: all six are OPEN for the United States. The photographs' licences are checked.
- Recommendation (the project owner decides): ask the Stiftung Arp e.V. (through ARS) for permission for the online
  lesson; or check each work's first publication in the catalogue raisonné (sophietaeuberarp.org lists the literature
  for each work), since a work first published in 1930 or earlier is in the public domain in the US; or take legal
  advice. Any other work by her raises the same question, so no alternative work is proposed before that check.

**Q6. Credits and the licence terms.**
- CC BY-SA 4.0 (read in the legal code). § 3(a)(1): retain the creator's name, a copyright notice, a notice that refers
  to the licence and to the disclaimer, and "a URI or hyperlink to the Licensed Material to the extent reasonably
  practicable"; "indicate if You modified the Licensed Material"; "indicate the Licensed Material is licensed under this
  Public License, and include the text of, or the URI or hyperlink to, this Public License". § 3(a)(2): "it may be
  reasonable to satisfy the conditions by providing a URI or hyperlink to a resource that includes the required
  information." § 2(a)(4): technical modifications "never produces Adapted Material".
- The lesson's credit for nos. 1, 3, 4, 5: "Photo: Paradise Chronicle · CC BY-SA 4.0", linked to the photograph's Commons
  page, which names the author and links the licence. The images are scaled down and converted to sRGB, not cropped.
  This most likely meets § 3(a)(2); the licence's name is not linked to the licence itself. The lesson is not an
  adaptation of the photographs, so ShareAlike does not reach the lesson.
- CC BY 3.0 Unported (read in the legal code). § 4(a): "You must include a copy of, or the Uniform Resource Identifier
  (URI) for, this License with every copy of the Work You Distribute or Publicly Perform." § 4(b): the credit "may be
  implemented in any reasonable manner". For no. 2 (Sailko) the credit links to the Commons page, not to the licence:
  § 4(a) is not clearly met.
- Screen 7 shows the six nature images, the Morris and the Taeuber-Arp looked at last, small and without a credit; the
  credit appears when one is enlarged, and the same image is credited on screens 5 and 6. Under 4.0 this is probably
  reasonable; under 3.0 the licence's URI should be with every copy (no. 2 appears there when it was the one looked at
  last).
- Finding: OPEN for no. 2; for the 4.0 photographs, a small gap.
- Recommendation (an app change, needs approval): link the licence name in the credit to the licence
  (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/), and give the credit
  with the small images of screen 7 (or a visible "image credits" link there).

## Sophie Taeuber-Arp (1889–1943)

The artist died on 13 January 1943, so her works are in the public domain where the term is life + 70 years, from
1 January 2014 (Switzerland, the EU, Israel). The six photographs were taken by visitors who published them themselves,
under a free licence or as public domain. None comes from a museum's own photography, so no museum or foundation rights
claim applies to them. Each one is a photograph of a flat work, and the photographer's terms are honoured: the credit
line under the image (gallery and screen 6) names the photographer and the licence and links to the photograph's page.
The images are scaled down and converted to sRGB; only no. 6 is cropped (see its row).

US note (all six): in the United States, a work by an author who died in 1943 is in the public domain if it was not
published before 2003, and so is a work published in 1930 or earlier (since 1 January 2026). A work first published
(for example, reproduced in a magazine) between 1931 and 1977 can be protected there for 95 years from that publication,
and one first published between 1978 and 2002 until the end of 2047. The publication history of these paintings has not
been checked. The estate claims rights in the US through the Artists Rights Society (see Q3 above, 9 October 2026).
