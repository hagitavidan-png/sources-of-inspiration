# Lesson 2.1 gallery images: sources and licences

Every image below was downloaded from Wikimedia Commons (upload.wikimedia.org), checked, scaled to at most 1600 px on
the long side and saved without metadata. Two images already in the site (`images/editorial/`) are used as they are.

The status records what was checked; it is not a legal clearance, and no image here has been formally cleared for
publication. **LICENCE CHECKED**: the source and the photograph's licence (or public-domain status) were checked, the
work itself is in the public domain, and no question is known. **OPEN**: a question is still open (see the row and the
list below); the project owner decides before publishing.

## Open questions (to be resolved before publishing)

1. Morris, *Pimpernel*: the museum names the Spanish ministry as rights holder of the photograph, and Spanish law gives
   simple photographs a 25-year right.
2. Morris, *Acanthus*: the image is the *Acanthus* velveteen (1876), a different object from the 1875 wallpaper first
   chosen, from a commercial scan with no museum record.
3. Sophie Taeuber-Arp, all six: the term in the United States depends on when each work was first published, which has
   not been checked (see the US note).
4. Sophie Taeuber-Arp, identification: no. 3 is identified with the Buffalo painting by its title, date, size and
   support (the museum's own image was not seen); for no. 4 the size and inventory number were not verified.
5. Sophie Taeuber-Arp, no. 6: the photographer published the photograph with a public-domain tag and no licence of his
   own; it is cropped to the work.
6. Credits: the CC BY and CC BY-SA photographs are credited under the image in the gallery (screen 5), on the
   comparison (screen 6) and in the enlarged view; the small images of screen 7 show no credit.
7. The two links of "Want to discover more?" (Kusama, Delaunay) could not be opened from the build environment.

## Sophie Taeuber-Arp (1889–1943)

The artist died on 13 January 1943, so her works are in the public domain where the term is life + 70 years, from
1 January 2014 (Switzerland, the EU, Israel). The six photographs were taken by visitors who published them themselves,
under a free licence or as public domain. None comes from a museum's own photography, so no museum or foundation rights
claim applies to them. Each one is a photograph of a flat work, and the photographer's terms are honoured: the credit
line under the image (gallery and screen 6) names the photographer and the licence and links to the photograph's page.
The images are scaled down and converted to sRGB; only no. 6 is cropped (see its row).

US note (all six): in the United States, a work by an author who died in 1943 is in the public domain if it was not
published before 2003, and so is a work published in 1930 or earlier (since 1 January 2026). A work first published
(for example, reproduced in a magazine) between 1931 and 1977 can be protected there for 95 years from that publication,
and one first published between 1978 and 2002 until the end of 2047. The publication history of these paintings has not
been checked.
